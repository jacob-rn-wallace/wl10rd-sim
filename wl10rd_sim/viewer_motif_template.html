What is from the paper and what is not

From the papers: link lengths, hip spacing, foot size and total mass (83 kg); 40 cm steps with 1.0 s single support; straighter legs than before (Fig. 18, and Figs. 7 and 10 of the WL-10R paper show nearly straight support legs; in this model the knees still bend about 45° while walking, down from about 63° in the first version, because the legs must reach a 40 cm stance); the CoM path over the sole in single support (Fig. 12, digitized); the three-state, sixth-order joint pattern with start and end acceleration, checked by ZMP; the tracking mode of Eq. 45 (sign corrected); four change-over phases switched by four micro switches per sole and the front ankle angle; ankle impedance control of Eq. 33 within ±100 N·m.

Estimated: the mass of every link (55 kg above the hips, 14 kg per leg, chosen so the CoM sits about 0.77 m above the ankle axis, as Figs. 8 and 12 together imply); servo stiffness; spike positions; switch threshold. The papers give none of these.

Changed to make it walk: hip and knee joints follow the measured pelvis during the change-over so the feet stay on their footprints (the paper says these joints do not move); the hind ankle roll is compliant in A and B; phase D also waits for the front ankle roll and a flat front foot, with a 0.35 s time-out; the stop step sets the swing foot down beside the support foot while the body is still moving, then settles in double support (the paper does not say how its walks stop). The change-over table and the patterns were tuned by search in this simulation.

Result vs paper: each change-over takes about 0.8 s, not 0.5 s, and phase D always ends on the time-out rather than the ankle-angle trigger. For most of each single-support phase the support foot is loaded on its outer edge, not on all four spikes. The CoM enters single support about 3 cm further inboard than Fig. 12. This 4-change-over walk completes, but a 5th change-over fails, and an extra 0.02 m/s of sideways velocity at the start makes it fall, so the solution is fragile.
